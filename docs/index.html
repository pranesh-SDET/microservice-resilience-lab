Academic & Industry Rigor

Why Netflix ChAP & SequenceFI Shaped This Lab

Instead of inventing ad-hoc negative tests, every fault scenario in microservice-resilience-lab maps directly to peer-reviewed chaos engineering research.

Netflix Chaos Automation Platform arXiv:1905.04648

Automating Chaos Experiments in Production (Basiri et al.)

Netflix demonstrated that happy-path integration tests hide broken fallbacks and misconfigured timeouts. Their ChAP platform automatically splits 1% baseline vs. 1% canary traffic, injects controlled RPC latency or failures, and validates steady-state KPIs continuously.

How We Applied It in WireMock

In testNetflixLatencyVariants_UnderAndOverClientTimeout, we benchmark both sides of our 1000ms client timeout: an under-timeout 150ms delay completes with zero retries, while an over-timeout 1400ms spike proves safe idempotent replay with only 1 ledger commit.

Read Paper on arXiv (1905.04648)
Temporal Fault Injection arXiv:2607.20050

SequenceFI: Non-Intrusive Temporal Fault Injection

SequenceFI reveals why fault timing matters far more than fault type in distributed workflows. A crash before a database write is harmlessly retried, whereas dropping a reply after a commit triggers double charges unless idempotency and Saga rollbacks are verified explicitly.

How We Applied It Across 3 Payment Windows

Using WireMock stateful scenarios (STARTED → CHARGED), we isolate Window 1 (pre-effect 503), Window 2 (post-effect CONNECTION_RESET_BY_PEER), and Window 3 (downstream 409 out-of-stock → Saga refund), mathematically proving ledger integrity via countLedgerCommits() == 1.
